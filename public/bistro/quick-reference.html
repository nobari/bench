Quick start

1. Weigh the ingredients

Weigh water and flour in grams on a digital scale with increments of 1 g or less. A measuring cup can give the wrong amount. Level the supplied spoon for sugar and salt.

Menu sheet · PDF p. 1 (1-L-014–019, 033)

2. Check the recipe and program

Add ingredients as your recipe directs, select its program, then press Start on the bread maker. Incorrect quantities or the wrong program can scatter or overflow dough. Dough on the heater can burn, produce smoke or cause a malfunction.

The sheet illustrates four mistakes: measuring only half the flour with a cup, replacing skim milk powder with milk without reducing water, doubling the flour, and doubling both yeast and sugar.

Menu sheet · PDF p. 2 (2-L-002)
Menu sheet · PDF p. 1 (1-L-038, 042–045)

3. Handle the hot pan with oven mitts

Always use commercially available oven mitts. Check the recipe’s removal and cooling steps.

Ingredient tips

Water

At room temperatures of 25°C or above, the sheet calls for cold water (about 5°C). Some programs, including Rapid White Bread and French Bread, always require cold water. When adding juice or milk, reduce water by the same amount. Program 8 has a separate lukewarm-water instruction below.

Menu sheet · PDF p. 1 (1-L-021–024, 027)

Bread flour

For bread flour, look for 12–15% protein (at least 11% for Japanese wheat). The sheet recommends Nisshin Seifun Welna “Camellia”. Seal after opening, refrigerate and check the best-before date. Use the flour specified by the individual recipe.

Menu sheet · PDF p. 1 (1-L-021, 023, 025, 028)

Yeast and prepared starter

The storage heading covers dry yeast, dried natural yeast and prepared starter. Refrigerate immediately after purchase, seal after opening, refrigerate and check the best-before date. Yeast is a living organism. Avoid poorly stored yeast: damp yeast may fail to drop from the dispenser.

Menu sheet · PDF p. 1 (1-L-029–030, 036)

Sugar and salt

Measure level spoonfuls with the supplied measuring spoon. Use the large or small end specified in the recipe.

Bread appearance

What variation is normal?

Room temperature, humidity, ingredients and timer settings affect rise, shape, crust colour and height. The white-bread illustration shows a smaller loaf at about 15 cm and a larger one at about 20 cm. These are examples. Results can vary day to day, and some programs make smaller loaves.

Menu sheet · PDF p. 1 (1-L-006, 008–013)
Low rise, but still loaf-shaped
Check for too little sugar or water. Weigh flour and water in grams. Was cold water (about 5°C) appropriate for this room temperature and program? Check the freshness of flour and dry or dried natural yeast. Low-protein flour may give a smaller loaf. Check for 12–15% protein in bread flour or at least 11% for Japanese wheat. The sheet recommends “Camellia”.
Menu sheet · PDF p. 1 (1-R-004–007)
No loaf forms · 60-minute bread
For this program, did you use lukewarm water (35–40°C) and dissolve the dry yeast at the start? See program 8 preparation.
Menu sheet · PDF p. 1 (1-R-008–011)
White, lumpy top
Check for missing or inactive dry yeast or dried natural yeast. Refrigerate after purchase and seal tightly after opening. Check whether the kneading blade was fitted or came off. Residue on the mounting shaft or in the blade hole can lift the blade during kneading.
Menu sheet · PDF p. 1 (1-R-012–016)
Sunken top with baked crust, or flat, square top
Both appearances share the same checks: too much water or too little flour. Weigh both in grams.
Menu sheet · PDF p. 1 (1-R-017–020)
Sticky bottom or deeply sunken sides
Did you remove the loaf from the pan immediately after baking and cool it on a raised wire rack? In a warm room, reducing the water by 10 g (mL) may help.

Source difference for review. This symptom row asks about immediate loaf removal. The instruction manual’s pain de mie removal sequence allows the hot pan to cool for about 2 minutes before releasing the loaf. The instructions are shown separately. Instruction manual · PDF p. 10

Menu sheet · PDF p. 1 (1-R-021)
Loaf rises too much
Check for excess flour, dry yeast, dried natural yeast, sugar or water. If using specialist bread-making flour, the sheet says this type tends to rise too much and recommends reducing water by 5–10%.
Menu sheet · PDF p. 1 (1-R-022–025)
Large hole in the bottom
The blade leaves an impression. Did you touch the underside of the pan while removing the loaf? Doing so may turn the blade and damage the bread.

Nogami at Home · program 2

Nogami supervised this soft, rich white bread for Panasonic bread makers. Its premium soft white bread has a different, unpublished recipe. The delay timer cannot be used for the Nogami recipes.

All three specify Eagle bread flour, Yotsuba unsalted butter, Sanonto sugar, Nuchima-su salt, 35% milk-fat cream and SAF Instant Gold dry yeast. At 25°C or above, use water chilled to about 5°C and reduce the stated amount by 10 g (mL).
